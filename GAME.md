# PHANTASMASCORE

A contraction of **phantasmagoria**, the 1790s magic-lantern shows where audiences paid to be frightened by projected images, with SCORE buried in it. Paying to be shown something frightening is exactly what this game is (D-009). `DINOSCORE RAPTOR CAPTOR ROGUE LITE` is only the repo name.

## What you are doing

You stand at one arcade cabinet, in one room, feeding it coins.

There is a handle on the front. You lay a coin flat into it, haul a spring lever back as hard as you dare, tip the barrel up or down, swing it left or right, and pull the trigger. The coin flips out and tumbles at a wall of open mouths. The small high ones pay big. The wide low ones pay little.

Miss them all and the coin drops to a wet table at the bottom, dotted with holes, with one slow arm sweeping across it. That table is a second scoreboard, not a safety net. Where your coin lands has already decided what you get; the arm only decides when you find out. You watch it settle and you cannot touch it.

The machine wants a number by the end of the night. Then a bigger one.

## Why you keep playing

Between rounds you get to cheat, and the machine lets you.

You rig it. Swap what coins you throw, change what the mouths are worth, bolt charms onto the cabinet, and above all get into the **operator panel**, the service menu where the owner sets what every target pays. You are not supposed to be in there.

By night six you have made a low hole in the floor worth more than the jackpot mouth, so now you are aiming at the floor on purpose, which is the exact thing the cabinet was built to punish.

**And you still have to make the shot.** That is the hook. Your build decides what a good shot is worth; your hands decide whether you get one. A perfect build with bad aim loses. Nothing is on a clock while you line it up: it is a pool shot, not a reflex test. When you miss, you know whose fault it was.

## What it feels like

A dying arcade at closing time, rendered like a 1997 console game that was slightly too ambitious for its hardware. Low-poly, wrong-smelling colours, a CRT that will not stop humming. Not haunted in a jump-scare way. Haunted in a "this machine has been here longer than the building and the staff do not talk about it" way.

The real cabinet was covered in dinosaurs. **We are not doing dinosaurs.** The four mouths are four creatures, each drawn from a B-horror film's practical effects work: TerrorVision, Brain Damage, The Thing, The Video Dead. Inspired by, never copied. The floor is original, one wet slab of anatomy with holes in it. And because the machine shows you things, the cabinet is polygons while what plays on its screens is grainy digitised video.

## Shape of a run

One night shift, about 30 to 45 minutes. Rounds, a fixed number of coins each, and a scoring window running down while your coins are still in the air. Hit the quota or the run ends. Between rounds: the shop, the dispenser, the operator panel.

**The quota is the difficulty. The machine never is.** The cabinet plays identically on night one and night nine: same mouths, same sizes, same throw. What changes is the number it wants, which starts easy, then climbs, then turns ridiculous. No amount of aim gets you there. Only the build does.

You lose. You unlock something. You go again.

## Where it is going

A paid Steam launch at about 7.99 USD, for the Balatro, CloverPit and Peglin crowd plus the PSX-horror audience. A free demo carries the marketing. Built by one person with Claude.

REDEMPTION ALLEY is the older sibling: same room, same rot, same grammar, a possessed skee-ball lane instead of a coin gun. Shared art lane, engine and price. They should read as the same cursed arcade.
